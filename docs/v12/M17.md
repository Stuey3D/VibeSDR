# M17 digital voice and data as a mode (V12)

M17 is an open amateur radio protocol for digital voice and data: 4-FSK at 4800 symbols a second in a 9 kHz channel,
frames of 40 ms, Codec 2 voice. It is used on 2 m and 70 cm through repeaters, hotspots and reflectors.

In VibeServer it is a mode in the same list as NFM. It is not a decoder window. The server takes the listener's 12.5 kHz
channel, runs its own M17 receiver on it, and plays the decoded speech instead of the FM audio. The mode is silent until a
transmission is decoded. The VTS line says what it hears:

| What the listener sees | When |
|---|---|
| `M17 · searching` | nothing decoded yet |
| `M17 · G4ABC` | in sync, G4ABC talking to everybody (`@ALL`, the broadcast address) |
| `M17 · G4ABC/P → M17-M17 C` | in sync, talking to a reflector module or another station |
| `M17 · G4ABC · encrypted` | an encrypted transmission: named, not played |
| `M17 · G4ABC → M0XYZ · data` | a packet or a data stream |
| `M17 · last G4ABC` | between overs: the last station heard |

What the station sends besides its voice follows on the same line (the app and the R36S) or as the line's scrolling text
(the web page): the relayed station (`G7XYZ via M17-GBR A`), the META text, a GNSS position (`52.6235° N 1.1235° W, 121 m,
49 km/h`), the newest packet (`SMS G4ABC → M0XYZ: Hello`, or `Packet (type 2, 41 bytes) from M0XYZ`), and the error rate
when it is 5 % or more.

There is nothing to choose inside the mode. There are no sub-modes, and there are no width sliders: the receiver takes its
own 12.5 kHz channel, and the server keeps it at 12.5 kHz whatever width a client sends.

## Where it is offered

- **Only where libcodec2 loaded** and can run Codec 2 3200 (the same library FreeDV uses, docs/v12/FREEDV.md). Without it
  the mode does not exist on that server: no switch on the setup page, no key in any client.
  - ⚠ **Decision for Stuart:** the callsigns, META and SMS need no vocoder. M17 could be offered as "data only" where
    libcodec2 is missing. It is not: a voice mode that is always silent would read as broken.
- **Only where the radio reaches 2 m (144–148 MHz) or 70 cm (420–450 MHz)** (vibe_radio_caps.h).
- **Never on an Icom:** its FM audio is demodulated and de-emphasised in the radio, and the receiver needs the channel's IQ.
- **On every SDR that reaches either band, the Airspy HF+ included (on 2 m).** It stops at 260 MHz, so 70 cm is never
  in its ranges and `touches()` leaves it out there by itself. Decided by Stuart 2026-11-16 ("M17 on every SDR that can
  reach it"); before that the HF+ was excluded on 2 m as it is for AIS. test-radio-caps and test-bench-hw check it.
- **On Android (the app's own hardware, VibeServer Lite) since 2026-11-20:** the APKs carry their own libcodec2.so (Codec
  2 1.2.0, sdr-kit/build-codec2.sh — FREEDV.md "Android and Lite"), and the app's server screen has the switch. Not on iOS.
- **VFO B is not available in M17**, as in FreeDV and DRM.

## The receiver (vibe_m17.h, vibe_m17_fec.h)

Written from the M17 Protocol Specification, **v2.0.10 (2026-10-16)**, spec.m17project.org. Every constant is quoted from
it: the sync bursts, the dibit mapping, the randomiser's 46 bytes, the CRC (0x5935, initial 0xFFFF), the base-40 address,
Golay(24,12) from its generator matrix, the K=5 rate-½ code (G1 = 1 + D³ + D⁴, G2 = 1 + D + D² + D⁴), the P1/P2/P3
puncturing patterns, the QPP interleaver (45x + 92x² mod 368), the LSF/LICH/stream/packet layouts and the META formats
(text, GNSS, extended callsign). The specification's text is GFDL-1.3 and its example code GPL-2-or-later; facts were taken,
no code. No other M17 implementation was read.

### The demodulator

1. The channel IQ (whatever rate the pipeline cut) is resampled to 24 kHz, 5 samples a symbol.
2. A complex low-pass at 6.5 kHz, then vibedsp's FM discriminator (NEON/SSE2), scaled so 800 Hz of deviation is one symbol
   unit (±1, ±3).
3. The root-raised-cosine matched filter (α = 0.5, 8 symbols), vibedsp's RealFir (NEON/SSE2).
4. **Sync and timing together.** At every sample, the normalised correlation with each frame type's 8-symbol burst (mean
   removed, so the carrier's offset does not matter). The stream burst is the LSF burst inverted, and BERT is packet
   inverted, so two correlators cover four bursts. A peak is interpolated to a fraction of a sample, and that gives the
   frame's symbol timing. The 192 symbols are read by cubic interpolation.
5. **Level and DC from the frame itself:** a least-squares fit through the 8 known sync symbols, then through all 192
   decided symbols, twice. The DC is the tuning error, and the report gives it (`off`, in 50 Hz steps).
6. **Following a transmission:** the next burst is looked for 960 samples (40 ms) on, ±3 samples, at a lower threshold.
   A burst lost in a fade does not end the over: the frame is still taken at its expected place (flywheel), and the
   receiver judges it by how it decodes. Five undecodable frames in a row (200 ms) end the transmission, as do the end flag
   in the frame number and the end-of-transmission marker.
7. **Candidates are ranked.** A frame waits 40 ms for its samples, and its own random-looking payload throws up about ten
   chance matches meanwhile. The candidate list keeps the strongest 48. With 8, oldest out, the real LSF was pushed out by
   its own payload every time (found by the first over-the-air test).

### Believing a frame

A sync burst is 8 symbols, and band noise through an FM discriminator matches one dozens of times a second. So nothing is
believed on correlation alone:

- **An LSF** must pass its CRC, with no more than 60 of its 368 channel bits corrected.
- **A late join** (stream frames with no LSF heard) must decode its LICH (four Golay words, at most 6 bits corrected in all)
  and its payload with no more than 22 channel bits corrected. It is not played until the NEXT frame arrives 40 ms later
  with the next frame number.
- **A packet** must pass its CRC. One joined without its LSF must start at frame 0 and be cleaner still.
- A frame whose symbols sit far from the 4-level grid (rms 0.75 of a unit) is not decoded at all.

### Decoding

- **Soft decisions throughout:** int8, the sign the bit and the size the confidence. The randomiser is undone as a sign
  flip, the interleaver by its own permutation (it is its own inverse), and punctured bits go back in as zeros.
- **Viterbi:** 16 states. The butterfly has one branch metric for all four of its branches, so one add-compare-select is
  8 lanes of int16: NEON on ARMv7 and A64, SSE2 on x86, and a scalar path that is bit-exact with both. The trace-back starts
  from state 0 (the four flush bits).
- **Golay** is decoded Chase-style: the 4 least reliable bits tried both ways, each test word corrected by syndrome table
  (up to 3 errors), the codeword closest to what was received wins.
- **The LSF from the LICH:** each frame carries a sixth of the LSF. Chunk 2 holds TYPE, so the voice can start before the
  whole LSF is in. The chunks with META (2–5) may change at each superframe, so each counter keeps its newest chunk and the
  one before, and every combination is tried against the CRC. A chunk damaged in this superframe is replaced by last
  superframe's.
- **Voice:** Codec 2 3200 (two 20 ms frames in each 40 ms frame), or 1600 for voice + data (its other 8 bytes have no
  application defined, and are not shown). A frame that could not be decoded plays 40 ms of silence, so the timing holds.
  Voice frames have no CRC in M17: near the threshold a frame the Viterbi got slightly wrong is played as it came out.
- **Encrypted** (scrambler or AES) and **data** streams are named and not played.
- **META:** text (up to four 13-byte blocks, joined by the control byte), GNSS (latitude, longitude, altitude, speed,
  bearing, radius; shown only when the position is marked valid), extended callsign (the originator, and the reflector).
- **Packets:** joined from their 25-byte frames, the CRC checked over the whole packet. SMS (protocol 5) is shown as text;
  any other protocol is shown as its type and size. Text from the air is kept to valid, printable UTF-8.
- **Not decoded:** BERT frames (an instrument, not traffic), stream signatures (not verified), the channel access number is
  reported but not used to filter.

## The protocol

- **hwinfo / vibeserver.json:** `modes` lists `"m17"` (see "Where it is offered").
- **Selecting it:** `{"type":"tune","frequency":…,"mode":"m17"}`, like any demodulator. `paramsFor("m17")` is an NFM-shaped
  12.5 kHz channel; its IQ goes to the receiver, and its NFM audio is only the pacing clock.
- **Report** (on the spectrum socket, whenever it changes):
  `{"type":"m17","state":"sync","kind":"voice","src":"G4ABC","dst":"@ALL","can":0,"ber":0,"off":-150,"text":"…","via":"…",`
  `"gnss":{"lat":…,"lon":…,"alt":…,"spd":…,"brg":…,"r":…},"msg":{"id":3,"src":"…","dst":"…","ptype":5,"bytes":23,"text":"…"}}`
  - `state` is `"search"` or `"sync"`. While searching, `last` names the last station heard.
  - `kind` is `voice`, `voicedata`, `data` or `packet`. `enc` (`scrambler`, `aes`, `other`) appears only when encrypted.
  - `ber` is the % of channel bits the FEC corrected and `off` the tuning error in Hz. Both move about once a second.
  - `msg` is the newest packet. A new receiver (a retune) numbers from 1 again.
- **Asking for it:** `{"type":"m17_status"}` answers with the report at once. It is read-only, so it is not gated on an
  ask-before-tuning dial.
- **Diagnostics:** `VIBE_M17_LOG=1` prints every frame the receiver is handed to stderr: its type, correlation, distance
  from the grid, tuning error, the bits the FEC corrected, the receiver's state and its verdict.
- **Switching the library off:** `VIBE_CODEC2_DISABLE=1` makes libcodec2 "not installed" for M17 and FreeDV alike.
  `VIBE_FREEDV_DISABLE=1` still switches off FreeDV alone.

## Threading and memory

The pattern is FreeDV's. The rule is Network > Audio > Spectrum > Decoders:

1. **Pipeline thread:** it only copies its IQ block into a bounded queue. Past 2 s, the oldest is dropped and counted.
2. **Worker thread:** the demodulator, the FEC and Codec 2 run on their own `vibe-m17` thread, at the audio-codec rung.
3. **Speech back:** the audio callback takes exactly the frames it sends. It is silent until 0.2 s is primed and silent on
   underrun. Past 1.5 s, the oldest speech is dropped.
4. **Bounded:** the demodulator keeps 3 frames of filtered samples, and the candidate list holds 48. A listener costs
   about 1 MB at most (mostly the 2 s IQ queue).

There is one receiver per listener on a range-locked radio, and one shared receiver on a one-pipeline radio or a shared
dial. A move of more than 3 kHz makes a new receiver; a zoom or a nudge keeps it.

**Benchmark row `dec_m17`:** one listener decoding a voice over from our own transmitter at 18 dB C/N, fed in real time.
Its rule is `"m17"`, primary and not a slot: on a box where it is not green, the mode is off by default on a fresh setup,
and the owner can always tick it back on.

|---|---|
| M5 Pro (test-m17's own measurement, no thread) | 0.11 % of a core for one listener decoding a voice over |
| Pi 2 / Pi 3 | not measured |

## What is verified, and what is not

- **Verified:** everything in the tables above, all of it on **synthetic signals from our own transmitter**.
- **The spec's own vectors** are checked where the spec gives them: the CRC, the address example, the QPP table, Golay's
  generator. **The spec gives no whole-frame vector,** so the frame layouts (bit order inside the LICH, the frame combiner,
  where the puncturing pattern starts) rest on our reading of it. An encoder and a decoder that shared a misreading would
  still pass each other's tests.
- **Nothing has been decoded off air.** No M17 signal has been received by this code. Stuart's UberSDR is HF only, and M17
  lives on VHF/UHF; the VibeServers were not touched. **The first real check is a real M17 radio or hotspot on 70 cm** into
  an RTL or RSP, with `VIBE_M17_LOG=1`. If it finds bursts but every LSF fails its CRC, the frame layout is the suspect; if
  it never finds a burst, the polarity or the deviation scaling is.
- **The SSE2 kernel compiles for x86-64 but was not run here** (there is no x86 machine, and the emulated container would not
  start). The test runs it on any x86 build and fails if it differs from the scalar path.
- **The ARMv7 NEON kernel compiles** (`-march=armv7-a -mfpu=neon`, in the armhf cross image) but was not run.
- **The R36S UI compiles and links** (arm64 trixie image) and its line is golden-tested; it has not been run on a device.
- **Not measured on a Pi.**
- **Polarity:** the receiver assumes the IQ's own orientation. A receive chain that inverted the spectrum would see every
  stream frame as an LSF burst, and none would decode. Nothing in VibeServer inverts it.

## Not built

- **TX** of any kind.
- **BERT** frames are found and skipped.
- **Stream signatures** (ECDSA) are not verified; such streams play normally.
- **Encryption** is never undone (the amateur licence conditions forbid it in most countries; the mode names it).
- **The 1600 voice + data channel's data** and **non-SMS packets** (APRS, IPv4 …) are counted and sized, not interpreted.
- **A list of past SMS** in the clients: the line shows the newest. `m17MergeMsgs` in m17.ts keeps a list (tested), for a
  panel if one is wanted.
- **iOS:** as FreeDV (no libcodec2 there). Android and Lite carry it since 2026-11-20.
